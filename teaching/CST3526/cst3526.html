This course is designed for third-year undergraduate students majoring in Computer Science. It introduces the fundamental concepts and methods of stochastic processes, with an emphasis on modeling random systems and understanding their evolution. Through conceptual explanations, mathematical analysis, and numerical examples, students will develop a systematic understanding of stochastic processes and a foundation for further study.

The course will cover the following topics:

  • Review of Basic Probability Theory: probability axioms, conditional probability and independence, random variables and distributions, expectation and variance, covariance, conditional expectation, and basic limit theorems.

  • Classifications and Numerical Characteristics of Stochastic Processes: index sets, state spaces, sample paths, finite-dimensional distributions, mean and covariance functions, and basic process classifications.

  • Markov Processes: Markov chains, transition probabilities, Chapman–Kolmogorov equations, state classification, recurrence and transience, stationary distributions, limiting behavior, and basic hitting-time problems.

  • Second-order Processes: autocorrelation and autocovariance, wide-sense stationarity, mean-square convergence and continuity, basic mean-square calculus, and linear prediction.

  • Poisson Processes: counting processes, exponential waiting times, arrival-time distributions, superposition and thinning, non-homogeneous Poisson processes, renewal processes, and an introduction to continuous-time Markov models and birth–death processes.

  • Gaussian Processes: multivariate Gaussian distributions, Gaussian conditioning, covariance kernels, and Brownian motion.
